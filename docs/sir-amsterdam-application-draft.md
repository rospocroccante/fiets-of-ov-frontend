## Startup name and one-liner

Fiets of OV. A trip planner that compares the bike against public transport for any door-to-door trip in the city and shows the difference in minutes, euros, CO2 and calories.

## The problem

Amsterdam wants fewer cars and less pressure on peak-hour trams and metros. Most trips that could shift to cycling never get reconsidered: people ride the route they always ride. For short urban trips the bike often wins on time once you count the walk to the stop, the wait and the transfers, and almost nobody does that arithmetic per trip. The information exists in journey planners, fare tables and weather forecasts, but no tool puts the comparison in front of the user at the moment of choice.

## The solution

Fiets of OV is a web app. You enter where you are and where you need to be, and it shows one comparison screen: door-to-door time by bike and by public transport, the fare you would pay, the calories you would burn, the CO2 you avoid, and the rain risk for the ride.

The design bet is that a single honest comparison for this trip, today, in this weather, changes behavior more than a generic cycling campaign, because it answers the question the user is already asking.

## Fit with the programme (wildcard)

We apply on the wildcard track. The app serves two goals the city has already written down: the modal shift ambitions in the Vervoerregio Uitvoeringsprogramma Mobiliteit 2025-2033 and Amsterdam's autoluw policy. A shift of even a few percent of short OV and car trips to the bike shows up as lower peak crowding on GVB lines and lower emissions. The app measures that shift per user, so a pilot produces its own evaluation data.

## Pilot proposal

One district or one large employer, [3] months. Participants plan trips through the app; we report aggregate, anonymized results: how many comparisons ran, how often the bike won, how often users report taking it. The city gets a tested instrument for fietsstimulering and a dataset on where the bike beats OV in practice. We get a launching customer reference.

What we need from the city: a pilot owner inside the mobility department, access to relevant data (bike counts, OV occupancy where shareable), and help recruiting the pilot group.
